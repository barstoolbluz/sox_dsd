**Outcomes wanted:**

1. Runtime dispatch in which a context selects its kernel family once, scalar
   stays universally compiled, and no hot loop pays an indirect call.
2. Both kernel families the SOW names, SDM and FIR, in isolated units across
   scalar, SSE2 and AVX1, each independently forceable.
3. AVX1 that is genuinely an AVX1 tier: compiled without AVX2 or FMA, runnable
   on a CPU that has AVX and not AVX2, and provably free of AVX2 and FMA
   encodings rather than merely intended to be.
4. Capability detection that cannot execute what the OS has not enabled, with
   OSXSAVE checked before XGETBV and XCR0 XMM/YMM state before AVX, and
   conservative selection on unknown parts.
5. Equivalence established on each family's own terms: the SDM spectrally and by
   state under analysis-v1 rather than by a raw DSD hash, the FIR against its
   scalar and high-precision tolerance and the one-LSB graph bound, and byte
   invariance across buffer sizes and threads within a kernel.
6. The existing gates still hold: 36 pass / 0 fail, `make check` unchanged, and
   P10's certified behaviour unaltered on the scalar path.

## What this host can and cannot provide

We run the hardware jobs. Two of them we cannot supply as stated, and would
rather say so now than have you discover it.

- The bench host is an AMD Ryzen Threadripper 7970X reporting `sse2`, `avx`,
  `avx2`, `fma` and `avx512f`. It can execute a forced AVX1 tier, but it is not
  an AVX-without-AVX2 environment and we have no such machine. The SOW asks for
  that environment and separately warns against claiming hardware performance
  from CPUID masking, so we read those two together as meaning a masked run is
  not a substitute. Instruction-isolation evidence over the built AVX1 objects
  does not need special hardware and is something we can run here.
- Per-family requalification of the one-hour soak is your criterion's
  requirement, carried into P11 by
  `tests/dsd/reference/p10_ratification_criteria.json`: "P11 requalifies every
  newly introduced runtime ISA/kernel family". From the P10 runs one family
  across the three native rates costs about 33 minutes of CPU, so three
  families is roughly 100 minutes. That is affordable and we will run it, but it
  is work for a later round and not something to assume discharged here.

## Where we may be wrong

Our reading of this tree and this host. We were wrong about the kernel family
last round and were corrected.

- We believe the throughput canary remains sensitive to link layout even after
  the alignment fix, by roughly 2.7% under an unrelated object's growth. A phase
  that adds translation units and changes linking order is likely to move it. We
  think that is a measurement hazard for your benchmarks rather than a defect,
  but it is the reason the backlog entry exists.
- We believe `dff_read` and `dff_read_fragment` carrying 4096-byte alignment is
  load-bearing for the current gate, so moving or reordering them deserves a
  measurement rather than an assumption.
- We do not know whether Linux reports OSXSAVE consistently in
  `/proc/cpuinfo` on this part; ours does not list it while listing `xsave`.
  Probing CPUID directly rather than trusting the kernel's flag list seems
  safer, but that is our inference from one host.
