## Implementation

**Reconstruction.** Four exact-rate private v1 profiles were registered in `src/dsd.c`, and the existing decimator grew to eight stages without changing the accepted v1/v2 coefficient arrays. A deterministic generator `tests/dsd/generate_p17_profiles.py` writes a versioned latency/phase/normalized-frequency manifest at `tests/dsd/reference/p17_profiles.json`. The emitted manifest records each bank in convolution order and all decimation phases.

- DSD512 mastering: 22,579,200 Hz input, 176,400 Hz intermediate, 7 stages (factor 128), native group delay 5,476 frames, pass/stop 40/80 kHz.
- DSD1024 mastering: 45,158,400 Hz input, 176,400 Hz intermediate, 8 stages (factor 256), native group delay 10,980 frames, pass/stop 40/80 kHz.
- DSD512 wideband: 22,579,200 Hz input, 705,600 Hz intermediate, 5 stages (factor 32), native group delay 2,468 frames, pass/stop 282,240/352,800 Hz.
- DSD1024 wideband: 45,158,400 Hz input, 1,411,200 Hz intermediate, 5 stages (factor 32), native group delay 2,468 frames, pass/stop 564,480/705,600 Hz. Its 705.6-kHz *public* PCM destination can preserve only 335,160 Hz under `adapt`; `strict` rejects the request. This is not a claim that 705.6-kHz PCM preserves the full 564.48-kHz input profile band.

The mastering final-stage filter is the immutable P06 `master64_256` bank because the final stage operates at the same **352,800-Hz** input clock as the accepted DSD256 v1 bank. Higher native clocks add exactly one or two half-band stages. The wideband final-stage bank has the same **normalized** P/S ratios across its new physical clock; each distinct full cascade is independently subjected to high-rate sampled response/alias tests. This is a deliberate, mathematics-based reuse of immutable FIR words, **not** a `rate <= requested` coefficient lookup or an assertion that a new continuous-frequency certificate exists. Existing bank hex words remain unchanged.

**Modulation.** Two explicit clock-keyed `encode.mastering.v1.dsd512` and `encode.mastering.v1.dsd1024` `sdm-8` *experimental candidates* were added. Generated `src/sdm_p17_candidates.h` contains checked hex coefficients and `tests/dsd/reference/p17_profiles.json` contains word digests. The unit-delay recurrence is algebraically invariant under physical sample-clock reassignment: for the same normalized input sequence, the state recurrence/NTF/STF and state units are identical, with state scale 1. This is not evidence of stable full-rate source interpolation, acceptable 30-minute DSD512/1024 programs, absolute physical-band distortion, or new certified state bounds. Unlike an accidental DSD256 fallback, the registry requires exact rate **and** ID; no production encoder path selects these candidates. The P17 composite profiles have `qualified=0`, no inherited guard-bound certificate, and no certificate hash. The normal `.5` PCM envelope and fail-fast/nonfinite latches remain enforced; the private test-only candidate constructor is the only entry for experimental measurements.

**Public safety boundary.** `dsd_rates[]` retains *zero* capability bits and NULL public profile aliases at 512/1024. Both DSF and DFF advertised writable rate lists still stop at DSD256; the planner checks independent READ/WRITE/RECONSTRUCT/MODULATE/DoP capabilities and refuses high-rate output. No high-rate native writer, DoP marker/transport mode, format handler or public default was enabled. DSD64/128/256 production profiles, PCM behavior and ABI/CLI remain unchanged.

## Benchmarks and physical limitations

`python3 tests/dsd/bench.py --phase P17 --results tests/dsd/results/P17/bench` exercises the inherited 36-case development profile and invokes `tests/dsd/bench_p17.py`. The new benchmark records **12** high-rate decoder comparisons (2 native rates × 2 decode profiles × 1/2/6 channels) and **2** private high-rate composite encoder candidates, with per-arm command/exit, FNV PCM/SDM digest, real native/frame counts, `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` DSP time, GNU `time` CPU/RSS where available, and atomic per-trial checkpoints. Packed and expanded must produce identical PCM bytes before a speed ratio is recorded. The logical reader→DSP payload reduction is approximately **32×** against expanded 32-bit samples; this is not measured DRAM/disk traffic. The benchmark accepts a configured **0.001–130 source-audio-second** duration, rather than enforcing the P16 short-harness 5-second cap. Its bounded repeated 4,096-frame source and internal encoder avoid file-sized memory allocations. Default P17 throughput samples are short, from a shared x86_64 AMD EPYC 9V74 host with five available vCPUs, not the operator's calibrated Threadripper.

The reproducible final benchmark invocations were `python3 tests/dsd/bench.py --phase P17 --results tests/dsd/results/P17/bench` (exit 0, `build/bench-p17-last.exit` and log, **36/36 inherited cases**) and the bundled P17 internal runner (exit 0, `build/bench-p17-refined.exit` and `bench/highrate-internal/paired.json`, **12/12 private decode comparisons, 2/2 candidate encoders**, each using 0.08 s of generated source, three timed repetitions and one warmup). Median packed/expanded private-DSP speedup ratios ranged **0.913×–1.181×**, varying by native rate, reconstruction profile and channel count. No generalized speedup, CI95 or regression claim follows from these short virtualized-host samples. Two composite scalar encoder candidates processed approximately **18.0 and 18.7 million native decisions/s** on this host, respectively; those timings include SRC/FIR/SDM and are not isolated SDM kernel throughput. The raw measurements, commands, RSS and digests are bound to the measured `dsd_test_p17` binary SHA-256 in the JSON.

Benchmarks are **development measurements only**. No real DSD512/DSD1024 source media, qualified long-lived encoded DSD, independent external decoder, hardware DAC, dedicated physical Intel/AMD hardware matrix, 30-minute/1-hour holdouts, sustained thermal stability qualification or controlled confidence intervals were available. The operator's earlier real-media P16 packed-vs-expanded improvements (DSD256 DSF 1.051×, DFF 1.084×) are background P16 measurements, **not** P17 high-rate results. The latest combined benchmark: exit 0 (`build/bench-p17-last.exit`), **36 of 36** inherited cases, P17 internal paired 12 of 12 high-rate decode cases and 2 of 2 private encoder candidates all PASS_DEVELOPMENT_TESTS_UNQUALIFIED (`bench/highrate-internal/paired.json`). Actual case-level timings and run settings are in that JSON. Earlier paired warmups and trials were also successful; the latest default 0.08 source-audio seconds is deliberately too short for any sustained-throughput conclusion. Exact receipts are recorded under `bench/` and `build/bench-p17*.log`.

## Evidence and next permitted phase

`tests/dsd/results/P17/numeric-p17/qualification.json` explicitly retains `rate_specific_profile_certificates_qualified=false`. The unrun gates are: independent commercial/reference DSD512/1024 corpus; cross-decoder compatibility and physical device I/O; continuous-frequency interval extremum/cascade alias proof (unless later engineering acceptance authorizes a different rigorously bounded method); full mastering/ultrasonic/baseband/reconstruction/SDM state and input-envelope matrices across extended durations; a sustained per-rate and multi-ISA hardware soak; calibrated CPU/RSS/performance comparisons; and container-specific high-rate writer/transport qualification.

**Release decision:** The P17 DSP source, private development tests and numerical candidates may be retained for further qualification; DSD512/DSD1024 production profile certification and native/DoP output remain **unapproved**. Additional P17 numerical/corpus qualification may proceed from this checkpoint. P18 can begin only under its own operator authorization and after the required DSP, container and transport acceptance; it must not treat this private development PASS as an automatically valid writer certificate. Preserve the P15 standing obligations unchanged.
