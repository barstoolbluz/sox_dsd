CLANS-8.** Both fail ordinary full-scale native nonlinear inputs. R1's
23-design selection was correct arithmetic for four insufficient native
screens; it was *not* a qualified stability-gated shortlist. The existing
CLANS-7 `encode.mastering.v2.dsd1024` remains the untouched, previously
qualified incumbent. No production candidate is selected by R2.

R2 expands the mandatory *research* screen from four to **12 distinct
inputs** (the four historical R1 screens plus eight new R2 stress cases).
Every one of the 55 design bindings has a complete R2 eight-case receipt set.
**36/55** pass all eight added native tests; **8 of the former 23** also
satisfy the original measured low-band improvement rule. These eight are
*preliminary investigation survivors only*; they are not an NTF quality
ranking or approval to change production.

The provisional surviving families are the DSD512-derived order-7
`d512-r5-a20` and `d512-r8-a20`, each with original, 0.25x and 0.5x
resonator variants; and `sdm-8-g0p25` and `sdm-8-g2p0`. Their ultrahigh-
frequency noise costs differ substantially, and no representative DAC or
physical playback constraints have been qualified. The hardware-specific
choice remains open. No other candidate is entitled to promotion on R2.

## F-02 — tonal replay analysis window: CORRECTED

The 1.2-second 20-kHz/40-kHz holdout tones have 25-ms entrance/exit
tapers. `REPLAY.md` incorrectly directed fitting from 0.4 to 1.2 s,
including the exit taper and causing an artificial ~189-dB spectral
residual increase. R2 documents the **actual original 0.25–0.95 s**
steady plateau and makes `study.py spectrum` infer/validate the
frequency/window from the original input SHA-256. Noncanonical windows
cannot be published as ordinary `screen-passed` spectra; explicitly
unsafe explorations are labeled as such. The primary 1.6-second 997-Hz
case correctly retains its own 0.4–1.2 s interval.

Independently rebuilt native streams for unscaled SDM-8's 20-kHz and
40-kHz tonal holdouts matched R1 packed SHA-256 exactly. A separately
implemented numerical reconstruction matched all original frequency-
band values; the corrected 20-kHz 0–40-kHz level remains
**-236.0423466 dBFS**, not the erroneous ~-46.81 dBFS produced by the
wrong window. The archived original holdout receipts were already
correct and are **unchanged**.

## F-03 — evidence semantics and numeric verification: CORRECTED

`verify_evidence.py` now checks per-source SHA/length, frequency,
steady-state fit interval, FIR beta/taps, halfband length, window type,
frequency partition, finite nonnegative power, Parseval-like energy
closure, the published grouped dB recomputed from every underlying
RMS band, and fitted fundamental amplitude. Adversarial unit tests
mutate the original source interval, reported low band, FFT parts and
filter parameters and require rejection. The original R1 verifier
now reports **historical receipt integrity only**, not candidate
screening approval.

`verify_r2.py` additionally insists on all 55×8 new native receipts,
single bound experimental executable, full profile/input/source
identities, real native PASS/FAIL evidence, unchanged 55 R1 source-
qualified spectra, and the actual corrected survivors.

Eleven SHA-matched native primary streams were regenerated from a
separately rebuilt executable. All eleven matched the original R1
packed SHA-256 and a **separately coded** decimation, sine+DC fit and
FFT band calculation reproduced the archived band values (maximum
observed difference: **0 dB** in this runtime). The two SDM-8 tonal
replays likewise produced exact archived results. This is stronger
than internal record checks, although it shares the authoritative FIR
coefficient definitions; it is not an independent playback oracle.

The large raw packed streams remain omitted from the release by
design. **Offline verification of a hash does not reconstruct audio.**
`r2_numeric.py` and `r2_welch.py` can be pointed at independently
replayed SHA-matched packed streams for additional true numerical
checks. `REPLAY.md` documents the complete procedure.

## F-04 — ultrasonic load and reconstruction objective: MEASURED,
## hardware constraints still OPEN

New `r2_welch.py` directly analyzes the **native 45.1584-MHz one-bit
streams**, using nonoverlapping 65,536-point Hann-window segments
(about 689.0625-Hz bins) from 0.4 to ~0.799 s of the *same* native
997-Hz source plateau. Results are integrated one-sided bipolar-bitstream
power, not reconstructed sine-equivalent residual. The analysis is
bounded-memory and independently enforces the bipolar ±1 Parseval
normalization (total power 1). All 11 independently reproduced primary
bitstreams have archived raw PSD receipts and native receipt identities.

The relative trends independently confirm the adversarial audit;
the SDM-8 variants exchange lower reconstructed residual below 150
kHz for materially more raw native ultrasonic energy. `evidence_r2/native_psd/`
contains further native 150–500 kHz, 2–5 MHz, 5–10 MHz and
10–22.5792 MHz bands, and diagnostic largest-bin locations (which
**do not certify the presence or absence of tones** at this frequency
resolution).

The scripts also report integrated power **above 150 kHz** after four
*illustrative mathematical* Butterworth magnitude-squared transfer
functions: two-pole and four-pole at 100 or 200 kHz. For example,
relative to CLANS-7, SDM-8×2 retains approximately +10.4 dB of
ultrasonic weighted power through a two-pole 100-kHz model and +12.9
dB through a four-pole 200-kHz model. These are **not** real DAC
measurements or acceptance ceilings. Real DAC reconstruction,
intermodulation, analog load/EMI, programme signals and output power
constraints have not been characterized. No design is declared the
best for arbitrary hardware.
