## What we want

The order study you ran at DSD512, run at DSD1024, and run wider than the
DSD512 one was.

`encode.mastering.v2.dsd1024` is CLANS-7, inherited from DSD256 and never
examined at this clock. At DSD512 that assumption proved wrong by about
32 dB in 40-100 kHz. We have no evidence either way at 45,158,400 Hz.

**Cover all ten production families.** `src/sdm.c` carries `clans-4` through
`clans-8` and `sdm-4` through `sdm-8`. The DSD512 search seeded from
`sdm-4`, `sdm-5`, `sdm-6`, `sdm-7` and CLANS-7: five of the ten. It never
touched `clans-4`, `clans-5`, `clans-6` or **`clans-8`**, and `sdm-8` only as
the defective eighth-order v1. If there is a reason those were excluded, say
it; otherwise include them. We raise it because it also means the DSD512
conclusion rests on a partial sweep, which you may want to revisit
separately.

**Seed from your own DSD512 redesign, not only from the inherited banks.**
`o7-r8-a20` was purpose-built and beat CLANS-7 substantially at 22,579,200
Hz. It is a more informed starting point than CLANS-7 for this clock, though
its resonator placement was optimized for half the oversampling ratio, so we
do not assume it transfers.

CLANS-7 is the **incumbent and control**, meaning the thing to beat. It is
not a reference in the sense of being correct or standard; whether it belongs
at this clock is the question.

Three things from DSD512 worth carrying in rather than rediscovering.

**Stability screening must gate fidelity, not follow it.** Seven of your
eight optimized DSD512 designs went nonfinite on a 0.95-peak 997 Hz tone, and
`order7-pareto-g3p0` diverged at native frame 10,129,308 despite an NTF peak
of 1.480 and a pole radius of 0.963. Sampled pole radius and H-infinity did
not predict one-bit robustness.

**The source working rate bounds what any shaper can mean.** At 176,400 Hz
input with the 61-tap prefilter about 209 dB down by 80 kHz, the ultrasonic
bands measure shaped noise rather than source content. Choose the objective
band knowing that, and say which band you chose.

**Say what would make CLANS-7 the winner**, so that outcome is a result
rather than a failed search.

## Two decisions that are yours, not measurements

**Is self-generated media admissible for DSD1024 read qualification?** No
DSD1024 media exists here. DSD512 read rests on 12 commercially mastered
NativeDSD tracks; DSD1024 read could only rest on files this encoder made.
That is circular, and it determines whether write must precede read or
whether read simply waits for commercial material to exist. We will not
assume it is acceptable.

**Should the DSD1024 finalist mirror the DSD512 choice or be selected
independently?** The clocks, oversampling ratios and bands differ, and your
own DSD512 report declined to generalise across them.

## What we have already established

`encode.mastering.v2.dsd1024` passes everything we can run: 3,600-second
nominal soak at 162,570,240,000 frames, min28 7, max28 21, longest run 5,
zero nonfinite, zero overloads, 6.93% guard utilisation; `verify` 17;
holdout 36 of 36; and a five-tier ISA sweep at full-scale DC both polarities,
10 of 10, 5.21% scalar against 5.22% vector, with exactly two distinct
bitstreams per polarity and the four vector tiers byte-identical.

Our 17 unguarded training cases reproduce your `training_max_abs_state`
exactly across all eight coordinates and therefore your frozen bounds. That
is arithmetic reproduction on different hardware with a different binary.

So this is not a defect report. The candidate works. The question is whether
it is the best available, and we cannot answer that.

## One harness gap

A delivered tree cannot be host-re-qualified from scratch. `freeze` requires
the guard header to still be the pre-freeze placeholder and `verify` requires
the original receipts, so the frozen guard and its receipts are inseparable.
Your DSD512 helper deletes both to sidestep this; DSD1024 ships no helper. If
host re-qualification is meant to be repeatable, that needs closing.
